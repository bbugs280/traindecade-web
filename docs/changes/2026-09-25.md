## Evidence (verified by live search before writing — the queue's own fact-check flags)
Primary sources confirmed:
- **Paluch et al. 2022, The Lancet Public Health** — 15 international cohorts, 47,471 adults, 3,013 deaths, median 7.1 yr follow-up. Quartile HRs: 0.60 / 0.55 / 0.47. Spline inflection at 6,000–8,000 (60+) and 8,000–10,000 (<60). **Stepping-rate associations attenuated after adjusting for total steps** and were not significant for 40+ or 100+ steps/min.
- **The Lancet Public Health 2025** — systematic review + dose-response meta-analysis, 57 studies / 35 cohorts. 7,000 vs 2,000 steps = 47% lower all-cause mortality (HR 0.53); 10,000 adds only ~10% more, with no statistical difference for CVD **mortality**, T2D or falls. 4,000 vs 2,000 = 36% lower.
- **The 10,000 origin** — Yamasa Tokei Keiki, 1965, `manpo-kei` ("10,000-step meter"); campaign tied to the 1964 Tokyo Olympics. Confirmed via BMJ and McGill OSS. Hatano's research came in the late 1970s, ~15 years *after* the slogan.

⚠️ **Two queue numbers corrected during fact-check (do not re-cite the old values):**
- The queue said the Lancet 2025 figure was "7,000 vs 2,000 = HR 0.53, 47% lower all-cause mortality" ✅ **correct** — but also implied the 2025 review reconciled to Paluch's 7–9k. It does not: **2025 inflects at 5,000–7,000; Paluch 2022 at 6,000–8,000/8,000–10,000.** The post states both rather than merging them.
- The queue's Paluch quartile medians were also checked and are as published (3,553 / 5,801 / 7,842 / 10,901).
