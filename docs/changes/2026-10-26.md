## Fact-check (Stage 4b) — primary sources only
- **Thomas K, Brownstein CG, Dent J, Parker P, Goodall S, Howatson G (2018).** *Med Sci Sports Exerc*
  50(12):2526–2535. doi:10.1249/MSS.0000000000001733 · PMID 30067591. Verified verbatim: fatigue
  resolved by 48h (JUMP) / 72h (STR, SPR); muscle decrements persisted 48h across all; reduced
  voluntary activation 24h (jump/sprint) and 48h (strength); conclusion "not primarily underpinned
  by decrements in CNS function".
- **Carroll TJ, Taylor JL, Gandevia SC (2017).** *J Appl Physiol* 122(5):1068–1076.
  doi:10.1152/japplphysiol.00775.2016 · PMID 27932676. Verified: voluntary activation recovers ~30s
  after maximal contractions; may not return to baseline until 20–30 min after sustained submaximal;
  central contribution ~65% at 5% MVC for 70 min.

⚠️ **Correction carried:** the candidate brief said central fatigue recovered "20–45 min per Amann 2016".
The actual 20–30 min figure is from **Carroll/Taylor/Gandevia 2017** (J Appl Physiol). The post cites
Carroll, not Amann, for that number.

⚠️ **Population honesty:** the 2018 trial used 10 trained young male athletes. The post states this
and explicitly refuses to extrapolate to men over 40 — the over-40 section is framed as
*practical implication*, not measured physiology.
