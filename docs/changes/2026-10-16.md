## The thesis

The deload post answers *should you back off*. This one answers the question it explicitly
punted: **you took the week — now what, and why isn't the rebound arriving?**

Spine: **the rebound is not automatic.** It has to be earned on the way *back*, not on the
way down. Villain = the calendar-based re-entry ("a week is up, back to normal"). Hero = the
reader who re-enters gradually and judges readiness by the warm-up, not by a wearable.

## Evidence (all verified live this session, primary sources)

| Source | What it supports |
|---|---|
| **Coleman 2024, PeerJ 12:e16777 (PMID 38274324)** | The load-bearing study. First trial to *test* whether a 1-week deload potentiates adaptations (n=39, 9-wk high-volume). Deload group: **no hypertrophy advantage**, **inferior strength** (1RM squat 85% posterior favouring continuous; isometric knee extension 92%). Deload group reported **increased soreness + decreased motivation**; participants felt lethargic, "out of practice." |
| **Rogerson 2024, Sports Med Open 10:26** | The failure table: trained too heavy 50.4% · volume too high 44.7% · **deload too short 32.5%** · fatigue too high 32.5%. The "too short" row is what connects to the Coleman result. |
| **Buchheit 2014, Front Physiol 5:73** | Resting (morning) HRV is the appropriate measure; HRV during exercise is not a usable monitoring tool; night recordings are noisy. |
| **PMC8346173 (2021)** | HRV baseline must be recorded **≥1–2 weeks** before making any training decision. No direct association between HRV and neuromuscular capability. |
| **Sensors / PMC12787763 (2025)** | Near-daily RMSSD (≥5 d/wk), interpreted as weekly averages + CV; relying on RMSSD alone is not advised. |

### ⚠️ One candidate datum was KILLED on evidence (do not resurrect)

The Candidate Queue's Candidate D (detraining) cited *"satellite cells +72%, myonuclei +13%"*
from Blocquiaux 2020 (PMID 32017951). **The abstract does not contain those numbers.** The real
findings are satellite cells **−30% (p=.054)** and myonuclei **−12% (p=.084)** on detraining —
*decreases*, both non-significant trends. That datum is garbled and was removed rather than
softened (Vincent's standing rule: citation uncertain → remove, never hedge). Candidate D's other
citations (Harris 2007: 4.5% loss @6wk, 13.5% @20wk, still 49% above baseline) **are** verified exact.

## Cover — 5 regenerations, resolved with a documented finding

DashScope **will not render an unloaded bar or a chalk-free gym.** Five attempts:
1. Racked bar, aggressive "no plates / no chalk / nobody trained here recently" negation → came back **loaded, with chalk dust**.
2. Same, made even more aggressive → **still plates + chalk**.
3. Bare-bar-on-wooden-floor + folded towel → **still 4 plates added**.
4. Empty rack, no barbell at all → **barbell + plates + chalk + dumbbells injected**.
5. **Workbench still life** (lifting straps + folded towel + water bottle on dark wood) → **clean, calm, orderly** — only a stray bar-end + faint chalk, which *match the thesis* (a session just ended, next one pending).

**Accepted at pass 5.** The prompt is logged in `scripts/prompts_dashscope.py` under
`after-deload` with the finding recorded inline. Vision verdict on the final: *"an evocative
contemplative pause... celebrates the dignity of preparation and recovery, not the violence of
exertion"* — an on-metaphor match for a post whose subject is the quiet re-entry.

⚠️ **Generalisable:** for any "rest / quiet / empty" concept, a **workbench still life** is the
only framing that suppresses DashScope's gym-scene prior. Negation wording alone does not work.
