I'll be fifty soon. I've been training for over twenty years, and I'm still learning. That's the whole point of this place.

Most fitness content is written by people trying to sell you a six-week plan, or by twenty-five-year-olds who've never had a real injury, a mortgage, or a decade of life get in the way of their training. I've had all of it — the torn shoulder that put me out for months, the plateaus that lasted a year, the phases where I got soft and had to earn it back.

And I'm still here, still training, still in better shape at forty-nine than most men half my age.

## What this is

Train Decade is a blog about the **long game** — building strength and body composition over years, not weeks. No crash cuts. No "transformation challenge." Just the stuff I've learned the hard way, through trial and error, over two decades in the gym.

The themes are simple:

- **Consistency beats intensity**, every single time.
- **Cut and gain are phases**, not events — they last months, not days.
- **Pain and gain go together.** Every real result I've ever gotten came with a cost. That's not a bug; it's the price.

## Who this is for

If you're a man in your thirties, forties, or fifties who's tired of the quick-fix noise and wants to build something that actually lasts — this is for you. I'll write it the way I train: direct, honest, no fluff.

If you're twenty-five and already shredded, you might find it slow. I'm not writing for you.

## Why "a decade?"

Because that's the only time frame that's ever worked for me. Not because it's a nice slogan — because I've watched ten years pass and seen what actually compounds, and what doesn't.

Small numbers, repeated, become enormous. A decade is coming either way. You might as well spend it building something.

I'll be here, writing what I've learned. Start with [Why a Decade, Not a 6-Week Challenge](/posts/why-a-decade-not-a-6-week-challenge/).
