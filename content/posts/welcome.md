**Train Decade** is a fitness and body recomposition resource built around one idea: meaningful, lasting change happens over years, not weeks. It's written for men in their 30s, 40s, and 50s who are tired of six-week plans that stop working the moment the camera turns off.

## Why this site exists

Most fitness content is optimized for clicks and sales — six-week transformations, crash cuts, and before-and-after photos that quietly fail once the diet ends. The advice is usually written for 25-year-olds on training wheels, and it falls apart for the rest of us: busier schedules, older joints, slower recovery, and a lot less patience for nonsense.

Train Decade is the opposite. It's calm, evidence-based guidance for men who want to train and eat well for the rest of their lives — not just for the next thirty days. Every article here is grounded in the scientific literature, not influencer hype, and every claim links its reasoning so you can check it yourself.

## Who writes this

The site is written by a lifter in his late 40s who has spent over a decade testing the methods he writes about against his own body — tracking training, nutrition, and body composition through cut and gain phases, and then checking the results against the published research. Nothing here is written as theory; it's written as practice that has survived contact with real joints, real plateaus, and real life.

That experience is the point. A lot of fitness writing is done by people who have never had to fit a training program around a full-time job, a family, and twenty years of accumulated niggles. The guidance here is written from inside that exact situation.

## What we cover

- **Training** — strength and hypertrophy programming that survives busy schedules, injuries, and plateaus.
- **Body recomposition** — the science of losing fat and building muscle at the same time, done properly.
- **Nutrition** — sustainable eating for cut and gain phases, without the diet-industry theatrics.
- **Longevity** — the habits that keep men strong and healthy into their forties, fifties, and beyond.

## How to read this site

The articles are written to be used, not just read. Most end with a concrete next step — a number to track, a habit to start, a question to ask yourself at your next workout. The goal is that you leave each one with something actionable, not just something interesting.

If you're new here, start with [Why a Decade, Not a 6-Week Challenge](/posts/why-a-decade-not-a-6-week-challenge/) — it lays out the philosophy behind everything else. Then follow the thread to whichever topic matches where you are right now.

## The core principle

**Consistency beats intensity.** The methods that actually work aren't glamorous — they're repeatable. Cut and gain aren't events; they're phases that last for months. Progress isn't measured against yesterday; it's measured against the trendline you've held for years.

A decade is coming either way. Build something worth having when it arrives.
